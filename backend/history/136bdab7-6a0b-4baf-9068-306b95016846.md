**Assistant:** **Article 219 – What it really means**

- **Who it talks about:** A person who has been appointed to sit on a *High Court* (the state‑level court in India).  
- **What must happen first:** Before he actually begins his duties, the judge **has to take an oath or give an affirmation**.  
- **To whom he swears it:** He does this before **the Governor of that State** (or a person who the Governor has sent to act in place of him).  
- **Why:** The Constitution wants to make sure the judge is completely clear that his only job is to follow the law, the Constitution and the statutes. The oath is written out in the *Third Schedule* of the Constitution – it tells exactly what he must promise.

### In everyday language

> “When a person is chosen to be a High Court judge, before he can start working there he must first say ‘I swear I will do my job only according to the law and the Constitution.’ He does this in front of the Governor (or someone the Governor appoints).”

So, Article 219 simply guarantees that every High Court judge begins his service with a formal promise to uphold the Constitution.
